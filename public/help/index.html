Getting started

What arrives, when, and the little you have to set up

Getting your first timesheet

Samay starts with an invite email. Once you have accepted it, your week arrives drafted in the channel you already use, and you can finish it from there.

Accepting your invite

One email, carrying an Accept invite button. There is nothing to sign up for and no workspace to go and find: Samay is invite-only, and that button is what brings your account into being.

  1. Open the invite and press Accept invite. It works once, and it is tied to your own address, so it will not let a colleague in if you forward it.
  2. Set a password, or use your company sign-in. Which of the two you are offered was settled by your organization rather than by you. Where it has connected Google Workspace or its own single sign-on, accepting that way is one click and leaves you no password to keep.
  3. Answer a few short questions. One of them is where reminders should reach you, and only the channels your workspace has connected are offered, so a short list is normal and not a fault.

If the invite never came, or it has sat there long enough to go stale, your administrator can send a fresh one from the Team page. Ask them before you write to support: it is one click at their end.

If a timesheet already reaches you

Then you are set up, and nothing else here needs doing. Your week arrives drafted on the day your organization chose, and you can finish it from the message without opening Samay at all.

What to do with that message

If nothing has arrived

Two things have to be true: your administrator has to have connected a channel for the workspace, and you have to have one switched on for yourself. The first is not yours to do. The second is in Settings, then Preferences, under Reminders.

Check as well that you accepted the Slack or Google Chat invitation your organization sent. Samay reaches you there, so an unaccepted invitation means a message with nowhere to land.

You cannot connect a channel yourself, and nothing in Samay will ask you to. Slack, Google Chat and email are connected once for the whole workspace by an administrator. If none of them is offered to you, that is the thing to chase, and it is a question for your administrator rather than for support.

What arrives, and when

One message, on the day your organization chose. It carries your week already drafted: your hours split across the job codes you are staffed on, drawn as a wheel.

It is a draft. Nothing has been filed, and nothing will be until you send it.

Why the first one often looks generic

Because it has one source. With no apps connected, Samay drafts from your resource plan, which is what your team intended your week to look like rather than what it was. That is why an unconnected first week tends to come out in neat, even splits.

Connecting one app is enough to change that, and it is the step the product never prompts you for.

A wrong first draft is worth reporting

More than any later one. It is telling us something about how your work actually looks that we got wrong. Hover any row to see what Samay drafted that hour from, and if the reasoning does not hold up, write to support@samayapp.co and say so.

What your organization set up for you

Samay is not something you signed up for. It arrived configured, and most of what shapes your week was decided before you opened it.

Knowing which is which saves you correcting the same thing by hand every week when the fix belongs somewhere else.

Decided for you

How you sign in
Either a password you chose when you accepted your invite, or your company sign-in where your organization connected one. That was settled for the whole workspace, so it is not a preference you can switch.
Your job codes
The projects you can file against come from your organization's own system. Neither you nor your resource manager can add to that list from inside Samay, so a missing code is missing upstream.
Which projects you are staffed on
Your resource manager keeps a plan of who is on what, and for how much of their week. It is why your very first draft had hours in it before you had connected anything.
When your reminder goes out
Your administrator picks the day and time, for everyone. You cannot move it, though you can choose where it reaches you.
Whether anyone approves your week
Some organizations switch on an approver step, and then a filed week goes to a named person before it is final. Nobody approves their own. Your timesheet shows which applies, so there is nothing to work out.
Whether time off arrives on its own
If your organization connected its HR system, leave appears on your week already accounted for. If not, you add it yourself.

Yours to change

  • Which apps Samay reads, all off until you say otherwise
  • What you tell it about how you work
  • Whether you enter hours or percentages
  • Your work days and usual daily hours
  • Which channels reach you

When the draft is wrong the same way every week

That is usually the resource plan being out of date rather than Samay misreading you. Correcting it by hand each week hides the problem instead of fixing it, so it is worth a word with your resource manager.

A code you need that is not in the list is the same shape of problem. Tell your resource manager which project you are on, or write to support@samayapp.co and we will chase it.

Filing your week

Check it, change it, send it

Filing from Slack, Google Chat or email

Most people never open Samay. The weekly message carries your drafted week, and you can read it, change it and file it where it arrived.

If the draft looks right

File it from the message. One press, and that is the whole interaction. For most weeks it is all it needs to be.

If something needs changing

Reply in plain words. There is no format and no command to learn.

Move three hours from Atlas to the EV launch.

Tuesday was all client meetings.

Make the Atlas number 20 hours instead of 10.

Samay changes the week and shows you the result. Then you file it.

Watch the total when you are replacing a number. "Make it 20 instead of 10" adds 10 hours to your week unless you also say where they came from. Check the total before you file.

Opening the full week instead

Every message carries a link to your week in Samay, and it opens you already signed in. Once your invite is accepted you never type a password again to reach your own week. The link also keeps working, so a message from a few weeks ago still gets you in: it is not single use, which means a mail scanner at your company cannot burn it before you click.

That link is you. Anyone who can read the message can open your week, so treat a forwarded one the way you would treat a forwarded password.

Editing a week in Samay

Open any week and it is already drafted. This is how to read it, change it and send it.

Pick the week

The arrows at the top move back through previous weeks. You can draft and file a week you missed, so being behind is recoverable without anyone unlocking anything for you. Forward is blocked, because a week that has not happened has nothing to draft from.

Read the wheel

Each slice is a job code, and the list beside it carries the same thing as numbers with the hours on each. The total sits at the top.

If you worked more than a normal week, Samay shows it as more than a normal week. It does not quietly flatten 50 hours back to 40. An over-full week is marked, not corrected.

Change what is wrong

Two ways in, and they do the same thing. Use whichever suits the change.

Drag the wheel
Pull any edge of a slice to move hours between projects. The numbers follow as you drag. Fastest when a draft is roughly right and two codes need rebalancing.
Type the hours
Edit any number in the list directly. Best when you know exactly what a code should say.

Hours or percentages

Hours by default, because most people think that way. If you would rather work in percentages of your week, switch it in Settings, then Preferences, or use the Edit % link on the timesheet itself. It changes what you type, not what gets filed.

One day at a time

If a whole week is too coarse, switch to filling a day at a time. Pick a weekday, fill it, and file just that day.

A filed day locks. You are filing that day, not saving a note about it, so file a day when you are finished with it rather than as you go.

Ask where an hour came from

Hover any row and Samay shows what it drafted that hour from, with its reasoning and a count of the activity it looked at. Use it when a number surprises you.

What Samay reads, and what it doesn't

Send it

One button at the bottom, labelled Submit. Nothing is sent until you press it, and nothing files itself.

If your organization uses an approver, filing sends it to them. If not, filing is the end of it. Either way you can usually edit a filed week for a few days afterward, for a period your workspace sets, so file something approximately right rather than leaving it open while you try to remember Tuesday.

If Submit will not let you, it is almost always hours Samay could not place. It says how many, and they have to be dealt with first.

Hours Samay could not place

Time off

Time off lowers what Samay expects of your week, so four days of work does not read as a week you underfilled.

Where it comes from

Your HR system
If your organization connected one, approved leave arrives on your week already accounted for. You do not file against it and you cannot remove it here. Correct it in the HR system and it follows.
Public holidays
Your administrator sets these for the workspace, so they are already on everyone's week. Also not removable here.
You
Anything you add by hand, which you can also take off again.

Adding a day yourself

  1. Open the week the day falls in.
  2. In the panel beside your projects, add time off.
  3. Pick the day, choose Vacation, Sick or Other time off, and set the hours. A half day is just fewer hours.

The note field is yours. It is not sent to your manager and it is not read when your week is drafted.

If leave lands after you have filled the week in

Samay does not overwrite hours you entered. It tells you leave arrived and offers to redraft the week around it, which is yours to accept or ignore.

Hours Samay could not place

Sometimes Samay finds activity it is confident happened but cannot confidently attach to a job code. Rather than guess, it hands those hours to you.

Why it does this instead of guessing

A guessed hour is worse than an unplaced one. It looks settled, so nobody checks it, and it ends up in a client report. An hour Samay is unsure about is shown as unsure.

Unplaced hours stop you filing. Samay says how many there are and how many hours they come to, and Submit stays shut until each one is either placed or discarded. This is the one place the product deliberately gets in your way.

Dealing with them

  1. Open the list from the strip above your week. The Review link on its right carries the count.
  2. For each item, pick the job code it belongs to. Samay shows what the activity was, so you are deciding about something specific rather than about a number.
  3. Discard anything that is not billable work at all. Discarding is a judgement, not a deletion of evidence, and the access log still shows the read happened.

Each row names the activity, its hours and the day it fell on, and under that, the reason Samay could not place it. A row is never dropped silently. Beside it sit an Assign to list of your job codes and a Discard, and choosing a code settles that row there and then, with no second confirmation.

At the foot of the list, All remaining sends everything still unresolved to one code in a single go. When a run of unplaced activity turns out to belong to the same project, which is common, that is the quick way out rather than working down the rows.

If none of the codes fit

Then the code is missing upstream rather than missing from your list. Discard the item so you can file, and tell your resource manager which project you are on.

What Samay reads

Everything starts switched off. Here is how to check

Connecting your apps

Everything starts switched off, and Samay never asks you to change that. So this is the step people skip, and then wonder why their week looks like everyone else's.

Switching one on

  1. Go to Settings, then Connected apps.
  2. Every app reads Off, Samay reads nothing from this app. Switch on whichever you are comfortable with.
  3. Approve the permissions your provider asks for. Samay asks for the narrowest scope that does the job, which is why the list is shorter than you might expect.

One app is enough to change the result. Calendar alone usually transforms a draft, because meetings are the part of a week that is already written down.

Switching one back off

Turn the toggle off and Samay stops reading that app from that moment. Weeks already drafted stay as they are, and you can edit them like any other week.

What Samay reads, and what it doesn't

What Samay reads, and what it doesn't

This is the page worth reading properly. It says what Samay can see, what it cannot, and how to check rather than take our word for it.

What each app gives Samay

Calendar
Event times, durations, titles, and who was there. Not the contents of an invitation.
Mail
Who a message was with, when, and the subject line. Never the body. Samay uses it to tell that you spent Thursday on a client thread, not to read the thread.
Files
The names of files you touched during the week being drafted. Never what is inside them, never your drive as a whole, and only the week in question.
Slack or Google Chat
Which channels you were active in. Not the messages.

What it never does

  • Run on your machine. There is nothing to install and nothing watching your screen
  • Track you in real time. It looks at the week it is drafting, when it drafts it, and that is the whole of its interest
  • Keep the raw material. Activity is used to build the draft and then discarded. What is kept is the timesheet, for a period your organization sets

Checking, rather than believing

On the same page there is an Access log. It lists every read Samay has made, dated, naming the app and the scope.

Samay read Calendar (event titles, attendees) on Oct 20

It fills in as runs happen, including runs you triggered yourself. If an app is off and its name never appears, that is the proof.

A row marked as failed means a read was attempted and did not complete, usually because a connection expired. It does not mean something was taken. If failed rows keep appearing, write to support@samayapp.co and we will look at the connection.

Where a particular hour came from

More useful day to day than the log: hover any row on your timesheet and Samay shows what it drafted that hour from, with its reasoning and a count of the activity behind it. That is the honest test of this whole thing. If the reasoning does not hold up, we want to know.

What your manager can see

Whether you filed, and the hours you filed against job codes. Not where any of it came from. Not your calendar, not your mail, not your files. The same thing they would see from any timesheet.

Settings and help

What you can change, and where to write when something is wrong

Your preferences

In Settings, then Preferences. None of it affects anyone else, and all of it changes how your week is drafted.

How you work

The one worth spending a minute on. A box where you write, in plain words, the things a calendar cannot show: which days are heads-down, what a recurring block actually is, how a kind of work maps to a project.

Mondays are deep work on the Atlas rebrand. Standups are 15 minutes, not the half hour they are booked for. Anything with Finance in the title is internal.

It is read every time your week is drafted, alongside your connected apps and your staffed job codes.

The rest

Input mode
Hours or percentages. Hours is the default because most people think that way. It changes what you type, not what gets filed.
Week start
The day your timesheet week begins.
Timezone
Decides which day is today and which week is this week, on the timesheet and in your channel. Worth checking if you have moved.
Work days and daily hours
What a normal week is for you. Samay drafts against it, so a four-day week set here stops Friday reading as a gap.
Hour types
Which way a draft leans when time could be client work or internal work.
Reminders
Which channels reach you. Only the ones your workspace has connected are offered. At least one stays on, and only an administrator can switch you off entirely.

Getting help

support@samayapp.co. A person answers, usually the same day, and you can also just reply to any email Samay sends you.

Two things in the message save a round trip almost every time: which week you are looking at, and what you expected to see instead.

Support and feedback are different, and we want both

Support is "this is broken". Feedback is "this is irritating and I have started working around it". The second is harder to get and worth more. Send feedback in your account menu goes straight to the people building Samay.

If your timesheet looks wrong

File it anyway, if it is close. An approximately right timesheet filed on Friday is worth more than a perfect one filed in three weeks, and a filed week can usually still be edited for a few days.

Then tell us what was wrong. A draft that reads wrong is the single most useful report we get.

When you cannot find a job code Not built yet

Asking for a missing job code without leaving your timesheet is being built. Today it takes one message to a person.

What to do now

  1. Put the hours somewhere defensible so you can file. The closest code is better than leaving the week open, and you can correct it later.
  2. Tell your resource manager which project you are working on, and that it has no code. They add it in your organization's own system, which is where the list comes from.
  3. If you are not sure who that is, write to support@samayapp.co and we will find out.

Why it is not just a button

Your job codes are owned by your finance or operations system, not by Samay. A code invented inside Samay would not exist where the invoice is raised, so the request has to reach that system. What we are building is the asking, not the inventing.

Starting a week over Not built yet

Wiping a week and redrafting it from scratch is not something you can do yourself yet. What you can do depends on whether the week has been filed.

A week still in draft

Nothing is locked, so you can change every number by hand. Set the rows you want and leave the rest at zero. It is more clicks than one button, but nothing is stopping you.

If Samay offers to redraft the week, that offer appears when your resource plan has changed, not as a general reset. Accepting it rebuilds the week from the new plan and replaces what you entered.

A week already filed

You can usually still edit it for a few days after filing, for a period your workspace sets, and that covers most corrections.

Past that window the week is closed to you, and an administrator has to reopen it. That is deliberate rather than an oversight: a filed week may already have gone to your finance system, and reopening it there is a separate step someone has to take.

What to do today

  1. If the week is in draft, edit the numbers directly.
  2. If it is filed and recent, open it and edit it.
  3. If it is filed and old, ask your administrator to reopen it, and say which week.
